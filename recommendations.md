# Global Electronics Retailer: Recommendations and Insights

# Key insights

Online is the strongest growth story, and for stores, productivity and growth separate the leaders from the rest, not margin.

1. **Online outgrew physical stores every year.** Online sales grew 18.7%, 86.0% and 51.4% in 2017, 2018 and 2019. Physical stores grew 4.4%, 69.2% and 40.7%. Online's share of sales reached 21.4% in 2019.
2. **Online earns the same margin.** Online and physical margins were never more than 0.6 point apart in any year, so growth is not being bought with discounts.
3. **Delivery improved.** Average delivery time fell from 7.3 days in 2016 to 4.1 days in 2019.
4. **Store margins barely differ.** They run from about 56.7% to 62.7%, so sales per square meter and growth decide which stores lead.
5. **Most weak stores are still growing.** Of the 10 stores weak on both productivity and growth, only Store 20 fell 10% or more in 2019.
6. **Computers carries the business.** It is about a third of sales and grew 54.4% in 2019. Home Appliances is about a fifth of sales and fell 4.7% in 2019.
7. **The Invest stores cluster in the United States.** It holds 7 of the 10 Invest stores.

### Refrigerators: Cut

Stop investing in Refrigerators and review its models, but do not delist the line yet. Its 2019 sales fell 15.3% while the company grew 42.8%, and its share of company sales dropped from about 4.9% to 2.9%.

- **Protect:** Fabrikam X9800 and Litware X980. Together they are about 58% of refrigerator sales at a 66.87% margin, and their combined sales were roughly flat.
- **Review:** the mid-size 19 CuFt and 9.7 CuFt models. They account for about 71% of the 2018 to 2019 drop, and Litware M760 alone fell 53.6%.

## Online store recommendation

Invest in the online store: it is the fastest-growing channel and shows no sign of buying that growth with discounts. Put the next growth budget into online marketing and fulfilment capacity, and keep delivery time under the 4.1 days reached in 2019.

- **Growth:** +18.7%, +86.0% and +51.4% in 2017, 2018 and 2019, faster than physical stores each year.
- **Share:** about 16% of sales in 2016, rising to 21.4% in 2019.
- **Margin:** within 0.6 point of physical stores every year (58.6% in 2019).
- **Delivery:** average time fell from 7.3 to 4.1 days.

The data cannot show process problems such as payment failures or abandoned orders, so those are outside this verdict.

## Decisions and assumptions

Every verdict depends on these choices, so a reader can challenge any of them.

- **Period:** 2016 to 2019. In 2020 every store and category declined, and 2021 has only about seven weeks of data.
- **Benchmark:** the median, not the average, because outliers pull the average up (the average sales per square meter is about 14% above the median).
- **Stores:** scored on sales per square meter and the average of the 2017, 2018 and 2019 growth rates. Margin is only a side flag, because it barely varies between stores.
- **Set aside:** stores without two reliable growth years, with no recent sales or with no sales data get no verdict.
- **Cut rule:** low on both measures and 2019 sales down 10% or more. The 10% threshold is the analyst choice.
- **Products:** categories and subcategories use margin and growth, split at the median, with a borderline band equal to 10% of each measure's range.
- **Refrigerators:** the rule-based Cut is kept after checking that its 2019 drop is not simply a company-wide pattern.
- **Online store:** analyzed separately, because it has no square meters.
- **Gap to median sales:** (median sales per square meter minus the store's) times its square meters, counted only for stores below the median. It is a benchmark, not a forecast.

## Limits of the data

The verdicts rank items against their peers. They are not proof that a store makes or loses money.

- **Profit is gross profit.** The data has no rent, staff or other store costs, so a store with a healthy margin could still lose money.
- **Relative labels.** Median splits place about a quarter of items in the weaker groups by design, even when the whole group is doing well.
- **Thin detail by store and product.** About a quarter of store-by-category combinations had $10,000 or less of sales in 2019, so growth rates at that level would be noise.
- **No process data.** The data cannot diagnose online payment or checkout problems.
- **Excluded years.** 2020 and 2021 are left out, so the verdicts do not reflect the later decline.
